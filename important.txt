The Strategic Content Focus
For college websites, your scraper should be laser-focused on high-value, actionable information:
PRIORITY 1 - Critical Student Info:

Admission dates, deadlines, processes
Course fees, scholarship information
Eligibility criteria for each program
Required documents and procedures
Contact details for admissions office

PRIORITY 2 - Decision-Making Info:

Course curriculum highlights
Placement statistics and companies
Hostel/accommodation details
Campus facilities overview
Faculty credentials (department level, not individual)

IGNORE COMPLETELY:

Historical events and news archives
Administrative circular archives
Individual faculty research papers
Old academic calendars
Internal student portal links
Technical administrative documents

Smart Content Detection
High-Value Page Indicators:

URLs containing: "admission", "fees", "courses", "programs", "placement"
Pages with structured information (tables, lists, clear sections)
Content with numbers, dates, amounts (₹ symbols, deadlines)
FAQ sections and prospectus pages

Low-Value Page Indicators:

Generated calendar pages (your current problem)
Pages with mostly links and no content
Archive pages with historical data
Procedural administrative content

The Real Game-Changer Approach
Instead of scraping everything, you could implement intelligent content targeting:

First Pass: Identify website type and map key sections
Targeted Scraping: Only hit pages likely to contain student-relevant info
Content Classification: Use simple keyword matching to categorize scraped content
Quality Scoring: Rank content by student utility

The beauty is that most Indian college websites follow similar structures. Once you crack the pattern for 5-10 universities, you can predict where the valuable content lives on any college site.
The Vision: A student types "VIT Vellore B.Tech CSE fees" and gets instant, accurate info instead of spending 30 minutes navigating through their horrible website. Multiply that across college research, and you're saving students hundreds of hours.
This is actually a brilliant use case because you're not trying to replace human interaction - you're eliminating digital friction that shouldn't exist in the first place.
